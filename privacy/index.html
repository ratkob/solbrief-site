SolBrief Privacy Policy

Effective date: 19 November 2026 · Controller: Ratko Božović · solbrief@ratko.biz

SolBrief is an iPhone app. It needs no account, no email address and no location permission. This page says what the app stores, what leaves your phone, and the two choices you control. Where something applies only from version 1.1 of the app, this page says so.

1. What stays on your iPhone

Your home town, the towns you follow and your personal reminders are stored on your iPhone. From version 1.1, the app also keeps a copy of today's headlines, weather and warnings for your home town for the SolBrief widgets, in a folder on your iPhone that only the app and its widgets can read. The widgets never connect to the internet.

To show the notices and events for your towns, the app sends the towns you follow with each request for the day's edition. Our server uses them to answer that request and does not store them. Your towns are stored on our server only if you turn notifications on (section 3).

2. No location tracking

SolBrief never asks for your location. You pick your towns from a list. From version 1.1, if you agree to Share usage statistics, Google Analytics works out your approximate location (the city) from your IP address, for statistics only (section 4).

If you agree to Share usage statistics, PostHog also keeps the IP address that arrives with each statistics event, together with those statistics, until you delete your data. The records above are linked to your random app identifier, not to a name or email. We also keep a daily total of how many times the welcome screen was shown and a first story was opened across the app, with no app identifier, device identifier or personal data attached.

4. Two choices that are off until you turn them on

Both choices are in iPhone Settings (Settings, Apps, SolBrief). You can change either at any time.

Share usage statistics. Only if you agree, the app sends usage statistics (for example which screens are opened, which stories you open, and whether you finish the day's edition, add a widget or open a notification) to PostHog on servers in the EU. From version 1.1, the same statistics and crash reports also go to Google Firebase (Google Analytics and Crashlytics), and RevenueCat sends your subscription events (for example trial start, purchase, cancellation, expiry) to Google Analytics. These statistics are linked to your random app identifier, not to a name or email. Firebase does not read your advertising identifier, and the app never gives Google permission to use this data for advertising. Google handles it under its own terms. Google Analytics works out your approximate location (the city) from your IP address. Google keeps statistics for up to 14 months and crash reports for up to 90 days. Nothing is sent to PostHog or Google before you choose, and nothing if you decline. Turning Share usage statistics off stops sending and removes crash reports still waiting on your iPhone. It does not delete statistics Google already holds (section 7).

Ad measurement. Only if you turn it on do we show Apple's tracking permission prompt. If you allow it, our measurement partner Singular records which advertisement led to your install, using your device's advertising identifier. Singular also receives what you do in the app (for example which stories you open or finish, and whether you start playing the audio), as events with no names or text, so we can measure which ads bring readers who stay. It also receives your subscription events (trial start, purchase and renewal) from RevenueCat, so we can measure which ads lead to subscriptions. If you leave it off or decline Apple's prompt, no advertising identifier is read. Separately, for every reader, Singular starts when the app opens in a limited mode: it receives your install with the usual technical details (device model, iOS version, app version and IP address) and its own random device identifier, but not your advertising identifier and no usage events. We pass that Singular identifier to RevenueCat, so purchases can be matched to the advertisement that led to your install if you later allow ad measurement. There are no ads inside SolBrief.

6. Why we may use your data (legal basis)

  • Running the service you asked for: the random app identifier, your subscription status, the towns sent with each edition request, your free articles of the day, and notifications if you turn them on.
  • Your consent: usage statistics, crash reports and ad measurement. You can withdraw consent at any time in iPhone Settings (Settings, Apps, SolBrief).
  • Legitimate interest: protecting the service from abuse (Firebase App Check and the hourly request count) and handling the mistake reports you send.

7. How long we keep it

We keep the records in section 3 until you delete them, except where the table gives a shorter time. Your notification record is also deleted when Apple tells us the push token is no longer valid.

Delete my data. In Settings you can delete your data at any time. This deletes your random app identifier and every record in section 3 linked to it, straight away and for good, together with your statistics at PostHog and the widgets' copy on your iPhone. Anything that still arrives for the old identifier afterwards (for example a subscription update from Apple) is deleted the same night. The app then starts again with a new random identifier. Delete my data does not erase statistics and crash reports Google already holds: Google deletes them when its retention time ends (section 4). It also does not erase what Singular has already received (section 4); Singular keeps it under its own retention rules. You can also ask by email (section 8). If you have SolBrief Pro, deleting your data does not cancel the subscription: manage or cancel it in your iPhone's Settings under your Apple Account, Subscriptions. Tap "Restore purchases" in SolBrief to use Pro again with the new identifier.

Our database backups are kept for a short time to recover from failures. Data you deleted is never brought back from a backup into the live service.

Apple and RevenueCat keep their own records of purchases, which they need for billing and the law, under their own privacy policies.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask to see, correct, delete, receive or restrict the use of the data linked to your app identifier, and you can object to its use. You can also complain to the Spanish data protection authority (AEPD, aepd.es).

You can delete your data yourself in Settings (section 7). To use your other rights, write to solbrief@ratko.biz. Because we do not know who you are, we need your app identifier to find your records. The app shows it in Settings. Without it we cannot tell which records are yours.

9. Children

SolBrief is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect data from children.

10. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy. The effective date at the top shows when it last changed.
